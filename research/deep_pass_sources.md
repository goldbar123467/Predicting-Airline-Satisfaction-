# Evidence review: training duration and ensemble weights

Research pass dated 2026-10-02 UTC. Owner: methods_research. Scope: primary-source review, current competition evidence, implementation inspection, and bounded recommendations. No real-data fits, audit evaluations, cloud jobs, production changes, or submissions were performed by this research agent. This note supplements the epoch and blend agents' executed diagnostics; it is not a new model result.

## Main finding and causal limits

The evidence does not support increasing the current maximum epoch count again. The completed 500-epoch experiment selected epoch 4 in each inner split and scored below its short control on every outer fold. This is evidence against that registered recipe. It is not proof that all longer training fails, that the model has reached an irreducible ceiling, or that a specific optimizer defect caused the loss.

Three mechanisms must be separated before another duration experiment:

1. **The schedule changes when the cap changes.** In installed PyTabKit 1.7.3, fit progress is epoch progress divided by maximum epochs. Our `flat_anneal` learning rate stays flat for 60% of that horizon; `cos_log_15` weight decay and `invsqrtp1e-3` dropout also use normalized progress. Cap 4 begins LR annealing around epoch 2.4; cap 500 begins around epoch 300. The first four epochs therefore have different regularization and schedule histories.
2. **Selection and refit have different schedule horizons.** Selecting epoch 4 inside a 500-epoch schedule, then starting a new four-epoch fit, does not replay the selected model's four-epoch prefix. It compresses its schedules. The new model can still be useful, but the procedure is not an exact checkpoint continuation or direct isolation of training duration.
3. **A real upstream decay-factor defect is present.** It affects the successful short recipes too. Changing it changes the model class as tuned and needs an isolated ablation. It does not retroactively explain observed AUC.

The long fit lacks retained epoch-by-epoch training/validation loss, checkpoint predictions, and optimizer states. Consequently we cannot distinguish classical overfitting (training loss improves while validation worsens), poor optimization, schedule effects, or checkpoint complementarity from its final metadata alone. “Selected early” is the supported description; “overfit after epoch 4” is not established.

## Inspected implementation and newly verified issue

The serious reuse candidate is the already installed [PyTabKit](https://github.com/dholzmueller/pytabkit), not an unpinned new framework. Git tag `v1.7.3` resolves to commit `c126ea51187c5080b91f28d352481dbd3b2194b0`. The downloaded optimizer and scheduler files match the installed files byte for byte. The actual [LICENSE.txt](https://github.com/dholzmueller/pytabkit/blob/c126ea51187c5080b91f28d352481dbd3b2194b0/LICENSE.txt) is Apache 2.0. Source, license, CI and a small ensemble-interface test were inspected; the upstream test suite was not run. Its declared floor includes Python 3.9, Torch 2, NumPy 1.25, pandas 2, scikit-learn 1.3, TorchMetrics 1.2.1 and Lightning 2. Those dependency bounds establish compatibility claims, not performance parity.

[Issue 40](https://github.com/dholzmueller/pytabkit/issues/40), created June 24, 2026, reports repeated learning-rate/weight-decay factors. The author acknowledged the behavior on September 15 and raised backward-compatibility concerns. It was open when checked. Raw issue and comment JSON were saved with UTC access times and SHA-256 hashes in `artifacts/research_pass_v1/sources/upstream_manifest.json`; this avoids relying on a mutable issue page alone.

In `optimizers.py`, `get_hyper_values()` first multiplies each parameter group's scalar by its `hyper_factors`. Manual decoupled decay then multiplies those factors again. For base schedule values eta and lambda and parameter factors a and b, the observed multiplicative update is:

`theta <- theta * (1 - eta * lambda * a^2 * b^2)`

A single-factor interpretation would use `1 - eta * lambda * a * b`. This is source behavior, not speculation. The epoch agent independently reproduced it using six zero-gradient scalar checks, and inspected the synthetic compiled architecture made from our frozen constructor. See `artifacts/research_pass_v1/epochs/synthetic_optimizer.json`.

Our active groups include first-layer weight factor a=0.25, PBLD numeric embedding a=0.1151, parameterized activation a=0.1, ordinary weights/embeddings a=1, and zero-decay biases. PBLD's decay factor is 1 here, not 0. A literal correction would increase the corresponding decay term by 4x, approximately 8.69x, and 10x. Bias decay stays zero; ordinary factors of 1 are unchanged. This is why silently patching the optimizer or upgrading the package is not a clean fix for generalization. The current hyperparameters were tuned under the existing behavior. A new correction test must record the changed effective dose and retain the old implementation as control.

Pure-decay retention calculations are diagnostics of the update equation. They omit data gradients, Adam state, and learned norms and cannot be interpreted as measured parameter collapse or a predicted AUC gain.

## What a correct blend amount means

For a frozen anchor b and candidate c, define p(alpha)=b+alpha(c-b), with alpha in [0,1]. AUC compares every positive-negative pair. Its margin is B_ij+alpha D_ij; only crossings at -B_ij/D_ij change that empirical ranking. Finite-sample AUC is therefore a step function, with half credit at ties, rather than a smooth concave weight objective. Many decimals in an optimizer output do not imply that the weight is statistically identified.

If two model columns are identical, only the sum of their coefficients matters. Highly correlated columns approximate that ambiguity: individual weights can move substantially while predictions barely change. Family aggregate weights, leave-family-out effects, and the range of coefficients producing similar held-out predictions are more informative than interpreting every coefficient as feature importance.

Local root diagnostics reinforce this: moving NN share from 47.57% to 50% added only 0.0000001314 development AUC. A 49-point block grid's best 65/20/15 versus 64/16/20 added about 0.000001583, with a negative third-fold delta. Best settings varied by meta-fold. These diagnostic changes are below the existing 0.00001 admission threshold and support a broad near-optimal region, not a new exact best mixture. They do not support deployment changes.

| Weight method | Useful property | Main failure here | Recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Frozen anchor plus one candidate, small preregistered alpha grid | One interpretable marginal contribution; limits selection flexibility | Candidate library may already be adaptively selected; best grid point still optimistic | Preferred next admission procedure for a genuinely new candidate, evaluated outside alpha selection |
| Few fixed family blocks, nonnegative sum-to-one coarse weights | Aggregates collinear members, bounded probabilities, stable interpretation | No benefit if all blocks are already redundant; fine grids reopen overfitting | Diagnostic now; freeze broad region and require reproducible gain before any change |
| Greedy ensemble selection with replacement | Targets AUC, sparse discrete weights; well studied | Large library and long greedy path adapt to OOF noise | Reasonable bounded comparator, not another unlimited search |
| Logistic regression on clipped logits | Smooth regularized fitting; can correct systematic score scales | Optimizes log loss, can assign unstable negative coefficients; meta-CV must contain base fitting/selection | One frozen challenger only if diagnostics show scale mismatch; never presume better from AUC invariance |
| SLSQP on raw empirical AUC | Generic constraints available | Objective is discontinuous/flat locally; finite-difference stationarity does not certify optimum | Do not use as proof of a correct weight; if optimizing log loss instead, disclose different objective |
| Unconstrained AUC search/CMA-ES across all members | Flexible target-metric fit | Larger selection variance and arbitrary extrapolation; published AUC overfitting evidence | Reject as default next step |
| Average percentile ranks | Removes each member's score scale | Discards confidence-spacing information; fitting/applying rank transforms across folds/refits can differ | Not justified by ranking metric alone; optional single registered comparator only with a concrete scale diagnosis |

AUC is invariant to a strictly increasing transformation of the **final score**. A decreasing transform reverses rankings; a non-strict transform can create ties. Invariance does not imply transforming every component before averaging preserves the blend. Probability means, logit means and rank means are different models. For one positive row with component probabilities (0.6,0.001) and one negative row with (0.4,0.101), equal probability averaging ranks correctly; equal logit averaging reverses them. Other examples favor logits. The relevant question is held-out marginal improvement, not a universal preferred transform.

A weaker standalone model can improve an anchor if it corrects enough of the anchor's pairwise errors. Conversely a better standalone AUC may be redundant. This motivates a **future** registered gate on incremental contribution, subject to fixed standalone quality safeguards and robustness criteria. It does not change the past standalone admission policy, reinterpret excluded runs as passing, or authorize repeating the closed saved-pool search.

The first three routes form the recommended sequence. Route 4 is a separate engineering/scientific ablation, not a presumed accuracy fix. Trajectory averaging is lower priority: current saved artifacts cannot supply the missing sequence, and the existing eight-member network ensemble may already capture much of the available variance reduction. A genuinely different representation or family should be considered only after conditional error analysis identifies an unresolved pattern; broad new-family sweeps or more seeds have not earned priority.

The clock must also be defined before refitting. Preserving an epoch horizon on a larger outer training set yields approximately 11.12% more optimizer updates per epoch here; preserving an update horizon changes the number of data passes. Neither is automatically wrong, but they answer different questions. The epoch agent's synthetic control prototype verifies prefix identity under one immutable horizon and a difference when the horizon changes (`epochs/control_prototype/verification.json`); it does not demonstrate a real-data score gain.

Subsequent mechanism proofs now cover best-checkpoint restoration, passive monitoring, checkpoint/export isolation and an eight-member generated-data mixed-input path with learned preprocessing. The last preserves a real monitor and literal epoch16 while exporting a cloned epoch4 graph without changing the continuing CPU trajectory. Six learned preprocessing tensors reside inside the vectorized network; the initially suspected repetition of nonidentity static preprocessing was not supported for the active PBLD branch. See `deep_pass_mixed_capture.md` for the executed contract and remaining production/CUDA limits. These proofs make the proposed experiment implementable; they are not competition-quality results.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Prediction snapshot ensemble | Compatible input/output contracts and the actual saved snapshot models or their keyed predictions; predeclared cycle/checkpoint rule and inference averaging | Existing independently trained outer models can be prediction-blended, but that already closed screen cannot become a snapshot experiment. Missing epoch models cannot be reconstructed from the final export. |

Exact training continuation additionally requires optimizer, scheduler/progress, RNG, preprocessing and sampler state, beyond the parameter state needed merely for averaging. The eight vectorized members must keep their identity across updates. A prediction ensemble across different seeds is valid under keyed prediction contracts; coordinate-wise averaging of independently initialized hidden units is not justified by that validity.

## Rejected claims and reproducibility checklist

Reject “500 epochs proves more training is useless,” “issue 40 explains the score drop,” “the official default 256 means we need 256,” “SWA can average the saved independent fold models,” “rank averaging is best because AUC is rank based,” and “an optimizer's precise coefficients are the correct amounts.” None follows from the inspected evidence.

The future comparison contract must record dataset/split hashes, preprocessing fit population, model/member seeds, package and source hashes, schedule horizon, actual update count, parameter-group factors, stopping metric, selected epoch, refit semantics, checkpoint/export identity, alpha search budget, and assessment protocol. This is needed to distinguish repeatable score changes from moving several coupled choices at once. Native inference equivalence remains necessary before any future release; this research note does not claim it has validated a new release.
